\begin{abstract}
Large language model (LLM) agents fail in a distinctive way: they
produce \emph{confident wrong answers}. Recent work has begun adapting
Byzantine fault tolerance (BFT) to multi-LLM networks --- notably the
weighted-BFT protocol of Wang \emph{et al.}~\cite{wang2025wbft} and the
Aegean consensus engine~\cite{aegean2025} --- typically by treating
agent trust as a scalar that flows continuously through the protocol.
\textbf{This paper takes the opposite stance: discretisation is a
feature, not a bug.} \mbft{} (Metacognitive BFT) commits a swarm
decision via a small, finite ladder of confidence tiers, defeasible
counter-proofs, and a reputation-gated veto. The resulting protocol is
auditable, deterministically replayable, and its safety / liveness
claims are mechanically checkable --- properties that continuous
Bayesian aggregators struggle to provide. Bayesian and continuous-trust
schemes remain the right tool for noisy, well-calibrated, high-volume
regimes (recommender systems, sensor fusion, market making); we argue
that high-stakes reasoning swarms --- legal, medical, safety-critical
agentic deployments --- belong to the discrete, defeasible regime that
\mbft{} formalises. We accompany the paper with an open-source
reference implementation whose property suite encodes each theorem as
an executable test.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

LLM-based agents fail in a distinctive way: they produce
\emph{confident wrong answers}. Classical crash-fault-tolerant (CFT)
consensus protocols such as Paxos~\cite{lamport1998paxos} and
Raft~\cite{ongaro2014raft} assume that any node which responds is
truthful, an assumption that does not survive contact with hallucinating
agents. We argue that hallucination is a \emph{Byzantine} fault and that
the appropriate response is a consensus layer whose semantics are tied
to agents' \emph{metacognitive} state rather than to bit-level log
agreement.

\subsection*{Design stance: discretisation is the thesis}
The recent weighted-BFT-for-LLMs line~\cite{wang2025wbft, aegean2025}
treats agent trust as a continuous scalar threaded through the
protocol. \mbft{} deliberately departs from that choice. Each agent's
metacognitive state is reported on a small, finite ladder of tiers
(e.g.~$\{\text{Low}, \text{Medium}, \text{High}\}$ or a bucketised
$[0,1]$); rejection requires a constructed counter-proof, not a noisy
scalar dip; commit is defined by a discrete predicate over those tiers
and over a reputation-slashing rule.

This is not a concession to engineering convenience --- it is the
central design choice the paper defends. Discretisation buys five
properties that high-stakes reasoning deployments need and that
continuous Bayesian aggregators struggle to deliver:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Auditability.} A commit can be replayed and inspected as
        a finite proof tree rather than a posterior trajectory.
  \item \emph{Determinism.} Identical agent reports always yield
        identical commits; no Monte-Carlo variance.
  \item \emph{Mechanical verifiability.} Safety and liveness become
        finite case analyses, encodable as property tests
        (Sec.~\ref{sec:guarantees},~\ref{sec:implementation}).
  \item \emph{Adversarial robustness without calibration assumptions.}
        Continuous schemes silently amplify miscalibrated overconfidence;
        a defeasible counter-proof either exists or it does not.
  \item \emph{Regulatory legibility.} A discrete commit rule with a
        named reputation history maps cleanly onto post-hoc audit
        regimes (medical, legal, financial, safety-critical).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{When the Bayesian regime is the right one}
We do not claim discretisation dominates universally. Continuous,
Bayesian, or stake-weighted aggregators remain the appropriate tool in
domains where (i) reports arrive at high volume, (ii) calibration is
empirically known and stable, and (iii) decisions are individually
low-stakes and amortised over many trials. Recommender ensembles,
sensor fusion, online ad allocation, and market-making swarms sit
naturally in that regime. We sketch a Bayesian variant of \mbft{} in
Sec.~\ref{sec:future} for exactly those settings, and ship its
reference implementation alongside the discrete protocol. The two are
complementary, not competing.

\subsection*{Contributions and scope}
We position this work as a \emph{refinement and rephrasing} of the
recent weighted-BFT-for-LLMs line of research, with discretisation as
the organising principle. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A discrete, tier-based formulation of metacognitive BFT
        designed for auditable, high-stakes reasoning swarms
        (Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol}).
  \item A defeasible-logic verification primitive that requires a
        constructed counter-proof for rejection, in contrast to scalar
        trust scores~\cite{wang2025wbft} or quality
        signals~\cite{aegean2025}.
  \item A reputation-gated veto: only un-slashed agents can block
        commit, so previously-faulty agents retain voice but not
        unilateral blocking power.
  \item Safety, liveness, and slashing-monotonicity propositions with
        sketch proofs (Sec.~\ref{sec:guarantees}), each encoded as a
        parameterised property test in the companion repository
        (Sec.~\ref{sec:implementation}).
  \item A complementary Bayesian generalisation
        (Sec.~\ref{sec:future}) intended for the high-volume,
        well-calibrated, low-stakes regime --- not as a successor to
        the discrete protocol but as its sibling.
\end{itemize}
What we do \emph{not} claim: novelty of weighted BFT for LLM swarms
(established by~\cite{wang2025wbft, aegean2025}); novelty of multi-agent
debate as a hallucination mitigation~\cite{du2023debate,
liang2023encouraging}; or novelty of confidence-weighted aggregation
broadly~\cite{wang2022selfconsistency}.

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Multi-agent debate and self-consistency}
Multi-agent debate frameworks~\cite{du2023debate, liang2023encouraging}
and self-consistency decoding~\cite{wang2022selfconsistency} aggregate
multiple LLM samples to reduce hallucination, typically via majority
vote or judge-model adjudication. These works do not model Byzantine
faults explicitly and treat all agents as honest-but-noisy.

\subsection{Byzantine consensus for LLM swarms}
The closest prior work to ours is the Weighted BFT protocol of Wang
\emph{et al.}~\cite{wang2025wbft}, which adapts PBFT-style consensus
to multi-LLM networks with \emph{dynamically adjusted voting weights}
keyed to historical response quality. Aegean~\cite{aegean2025} ships
a production BFT engine for multi-agent LLM systems with adaptive
quorum detection and AutoGen integration. Both treat agent trust as a
scalar; both use vote-counting (weighted or unweighted) as the
verification primitive.

\subsection{Classical consensus}
Paxos~\cite{lamport1998paxos} and Raft~\cite{ongaro2014raft} are
crash-fault-tolerant and assume any responding node is truthful, an
assumption violated by hallucinating LLMs. PBFT~\cite{castro1999pbft}
and HotStuff~\cite{yin2019hotstuff} provide Byzantine guarantees but
operate over bit-level state rather than over reasoning artefacts.

\subsection{Where \mbft{} sits}
\mbft{} is best understood as a logic-flavoured refinement of
\cite{wang2025wbft}. The novel mechanisms are (i) a defeasible-proof
verification step in place of a scalar quality signal, (ii) a
reputation-gated veto rule, and (iii) a mechanically checked property
suite shipped with the paper. We do not benchmark against
\cite{wang2025wbft, aegean2025}; a head-to-head empirical comparison
is the obvious next step and is left as future work.

\section{The mBFT Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\subsection{Ontology}
\begin{definition}[Agent set]
Let $\agentset = \{a_1, \dots, a_n\}$ be a finite set of agents. A
non-empty subset $\mathcal{B} \subset \agentset$ may be Byzantine; let
$f = |\mathcal{B}|$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Metacognitive state]
For task $T$ and proposed solution $S_i$, agent $a_i$ produces a
\emph{metacognitive weight} $\tauconf_i(S_i) \in [0,1]$ and a proof
trace $P_i$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Reputation]
Each agent carries a reputation $\rho_i \in (0, 1]$, initialised to $1$
and reduced by a multiplicative \emph{slash factor} $\sigma \in (0,1)$
whenever it leads a failed round.
\end{definition}

\subsection{State transitions}

\paragraph*{Epistemic Leader Election.}
\begin{equation}
L_r = \arg\max_{i \in \{1,\dots,n\}} \rho_i \cdot \tauconf_i(S_i).
\label{eq:leader}
\end{equation}

\paragraph*{Semantic Verification.}
Each follower $a_i$ executes a verifier $\mathrm{Verify}_i(P_L)$ and
emits a vote
\begin{equation}
V_i(S_L) =
\begin{cases}
\rho_i \cdot \tauconf_i(S_L) & \text{if } \mathrm{Verify}_i(P_L) = \top \\
-\rho_i \cdot \tauconf_i(\neg S_L) & \text{if counter-proof produced} \\
0 & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:vote}
\end{equation}

\paragraph*{Confidence-Weighted Finality.}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Commit}(S_L) \iff
&\Bigl(\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{n} V_i(S_L) \ge \thetameta\Bigr) \\
&\wedge\;\bigl(\nexists\, i \colon V_i < 0 \wedge \rho_i = 1\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:commit}
\end{equation}

\paragraph*{View Change.}
On commit failure, $\rho_{L_r} \mathrel{:=} \sigma \cdot \rho_{L_r}$ and
the strongest reputation-weighted counter-proposer leads round $r+1$.

\subsection{Algorithm}
\begin{figure}[t]
\hrule\vspace{2pt}
\textbf{Algorithm 1: \mbft{} round}\\[-4pt]
\hrule\vspace{4pt}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State each $a_i$ produces $(S_i, P_i, \tauconf_i)$ in parallel
\State elect $L_r$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:leader}
\State each follower computes $V_i$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:vote}
\If{Eq.~\eqref{eq:commit} holds} \State \Return $S_{L_r}$
\Else \State slash leader; promote counter-proposer; recurse
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\vspace{2pt}\hrule
\label{alg:mbft}
\end{figure}

\section{Theoretical Guarantees}
\label{sec:guarantees}

\begin{proposition}[Safety under $f < n/3$]
\label{prop:safety}
Let $f < n/3$ and let $\thetameta$ satisfy
$\thetameta > f \cdot \tauconf_{\max}$. Then \mbft{} never commits a
proposal originating from $\mathcal{B}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
A Byzantine leader cannot accumulate $\thetameta$ without recruiting at
least one honest vote, but honest verifiers reject any unsound proof and
emit a counter-proof, triggering the veto clause of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:commit}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Liveness under unanimity]
\label{prop:liveness}
If all $n$ agents are honest and propose the same $S$ with mean
confidence $\bar\tau$, and $\thetameta \le n\bar\tau$, then \mbft{}
commits in round $0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Slashing monotonicity]
\label{prop:monotone}
For every agent $a_i$ and every round $r$, $\rho_i^{(r+1)} \le
\rho_i^{(r)}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Wisdom-of-the-swarm]
\label{prop:wisdom}
Holding $\bar\tau$ fixed, the committed aggregate weight is
non-decreasing in $n$.
\end{proposition}

Each proposition is encoded as a parameterised property test in
\texttt{tests/test\_propositions.py}; see Sec.~\ref{sec:implementation}.

\section{Discussion and Future Work}
\label{sec:future}

\paragraph*{The Bayesian sibling, and where it belongs.}
The discretisation defended in Sec.~\ref{sec:intro} is the right
default for high-stakes reasoning swarms, but it is not the only
useful regime. For domains with high report volume, empirically
stable calibration, and low individual stakes --- recommender
ensembles, sensor fusion, online bidding, market making --- a
continuous Bayesian aggregator is more natural and more sample
efficient. We sketch such a variant here. Each report
$(S_i, \tauconf_i)$ becomes a likelihood signal:
\[
P(\text{report}_i \mid h) =
\begin{cases}
\tauconf_i & \text{if } S_i = h \\
(1-\tauconf_i)/(k-1) & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\]
with reputation acting as a tempering exponent. Commit requires both a
posterior-mass threshold and a Bayes-factor margin. A reference
implementation lives in \texttt{src/core/protocol\_bayesian.py}. We
emphasise that this variant is a \emph{sibling}, not a successor: the
two protocols target different regimes and the discrete \mbft{} commit
rule is what we defend for reasoning-critical deployments.

\paragraph*{Open problems.}
(i) Calibration of $\tauconf$ from raw token log-probabilities ---
acute for the Bayesian sibling, less so for the discrete tiered form;
(ii) modelling correlated agent errors that violate the independence
assumption of the Bayesian variant;
(iii) pipelined view changes, in the spirit of HotStuff, to amortise
the cost of leader rotation;
(iv) a head-to-head empirical comparison against
WBFT~\cite{wang2025wbft} and Aegean~\cite{aegean2025} on a
reasoning-heavy benchmark.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\mbft{} stakes out a deliberately discrete, tier-based corner of the
weighted-BFT-for-LLMs design space~\cite{wang2025wbft, aegean2025}.
The wager is that auditability, determinism, and mechanical
verifiability matter more than continuous calibration in the domains
where consensus on machine reasoning will actually be deployed under
scrutiny --- legal, medical, financial, safety-critical. Where those
constraints relax, the Bayesian sibling sketched in
Sec.~\ref{sec:future} is the right tool. The two protocols partition
the space; neither subsumes the other.
